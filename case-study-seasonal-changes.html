When the Seasons Shift, So Does Recovery

How one addiction-recovery program's data uncovered a pattern no one was looking for — a Michael Data Solutions case study.

Addiction recovery remains one of the least visible corners of American healthcare, even as the disease touches nearly every family in the country. Dr. Roop, founder of the Center for Trait-Based Transformation, has spent his career changing that. Working out of Kentucky, he built a program that identifies the strong personal traits already present in each person struggling with addiction and uses them as the foundation for recovery. The approach has since expanded well beyond his home state, now adopted by schools, treatment centers, universities, and workplaces across the United States.

The Challenge

As the program grew, so did the volume of data behind it. Dr. Roop found himself unable to translate that data into a clear picture of patient behavior over time. His own clients, in turn, were asking for something he did not yet have: a live, automated dashboard that could visualize outcomes while guaranteeing that sensitive client information remained secure. He approached Michael Data Solutions for a way forward.

The Solution

Protecting patient privacy came first. Before any development began, Dr. Roop supplied a fully de-identified data template, and the team studied its structure to understand how patient information was recorded and organized. From there, a custom dashboard took shape, built to track both trait-level progress and behavioral markers such as anxiety and depression. The finished product gave Dr. Roop two vantage points at once: a granular view into any single patient's development, and a broader view of outcomes across his entire organization.

An Unexpected Discovery

The most striking result of the project was one nobody had set out to find. Among the dashboard's features was a time-lapse visualization tracking trait fluctuations by season across multiple years, built originally to demonstrate the program's cumulative impact. It did that, but it also revealed something more: a measurable relationship between seasonal change and behavioral patterns, including shifts in relapse risk, motivation, and emotional stability. The same visualization let Dr. Roop trace how his program's effectiveness had evolved over time, rising, dipping, and ultimately settling into a consistent, positive trend.

A dashboard built to report on outcomes ended up surfacing a measurable link between seasonal change and relapse risk, motivation, and emotional stability — a pattern nobody had set out to find.
